Descendants

Below are the characters and their mythological, folkloric, and literary influences:

Mal

- Sleeping Beauty (Charles Perrault and the Brothers Grimm): Mal is the daughter of Maleficent, a character associated with these versions of the tale. The character of Maleficent also draws from the 14th-century medieval romance Perceforest, the 1634 story Sun, Moon, and Talia, as well as Gothic art and Czechoslovak medieval art.
- Greco-Roman Mythology: As the daughter of Hades, Mal has a connection to the Greek god of the underworld.

Evie

- Snow White (Brothers Grimm): Evie is the daughter of the Evil Queen. The character incorporates references to the tale of Richilde (1782), German oral tradition, and historical figures such as Claudia Elisabeth von Venningen, Countess of Reichenstein, and Katharina von Hatzfeld.
- Judeo-Christian Mythology: Her name alludes to Eve, a figure associated with the forbidden fruit—often depicted as an apple.

Uma

- The Little Mermaid (Hans Christian Andersen): Uma is the daughter of Ursula—a character inspired by the sea witch from the original tale—and also incorporates references to the drag queen Divine.
- Peter Pan (J. M. Barrie): Her association with Captain Hook’s crew ties into the pirate-themed world of the story.
- Hindu Mythology: Her name is derived from "Uma," one of the names of the goddess Parvati, who is associated with strength, devotion, and primordial power. In Sanskrit, the name carries meanings such as light and splendor.
- British Folklore: Her connection to the sea can also be likened to the sea witches found in Scottish and English traditions, including those featured in the accounts of the North Berwick witch trials.
- Greco-Roman Mythology: As a cecaelia (half-human, half-octopus), Uma is reminiscent of marine creatures such as Scylla and the sirens from Homer’s Odyssey.
