Pokémon

Below are the characters and their mythological, folkloric, and literary influences:

Bulbasaur

- The Vegetable Lamb of Tartary: Bulbasaur's combination of an animal and a plant evokes the legendary Vegetable Lamb of Tartary, a medieval myth about a plant that produced fruits resembling animals.
- The World Turtle: Its design can also be associated with the mythological concept of the World Turtle, a giant turtle believed to carry or support the natural world on its back.
- Dicynodonts: Despite its dinosaur-like appearance, Bulbasaur's body has been compared to prehistoric mammal-like reptiles such as Lystrosaurus, a dicynodont that lived during the Permian period, before dinosaurs appeared.
- Rafflesia arnoldii: The large plant growing on Venusaur's back resembles the Rafflesia arnoldii, a parasitic plant native to Southeast Asian rainforests, famous for producing one of the world's largest individual flowers. Its strong odor attracts insects, while Venusaur's flower is associated with a sweet and pleasant fragrance.
- Symbiosis: The Bulbasaur evolutionary line represents a relationship between an animal and a plant, which grow together and become increasingly dependent on one another.

Charmander

- Western Dragons: Charizard's design draws from the traditional image of Western dragons, with wings, scales, a long tail, and the ability to breathe fire.
- European Folklore: Charmander's tail flame evokes the mythological salamander, a creature from European alchemical and natural traditions believed to live in or withstand fire without being burned. In Pokémon, the flame on Charmander's tail is directly connected to its life force.
- Kaiju and Japanese Cinema: Charizard and Charmeleon's designs evoke the giant monsters of Japanese cinema, particularly Godzilla, through their bipedal posture, powerful tails, and fire-breathing abilities.
- Alchemy and the Great Work: Their evolutionary progression can be interpreted through the symbolism of alchemical transformation. Charmeleon's red coloration recalls Rubedo, the reddening stage associated with fire, passion, and the completion of the alchemical process.

Squirtle

- Japanese Folklore: Squirtle's turtle design and Wartortle's features evoke the Minogame, a mythical Japanese turtle believed to live for a thousand years. According to tradition, it develops a long tail made of seaweed, symbolizing longevity, wisdom, and good fortune.
- Wartortle's Appearance: Its furry features around the ears and tail resemble the white, cloud-like tufts traditionally associated with the Minogame. This connection is reinforced by Wartortle's association with long life and its Pokédex descriptions.
- Military Technology: Blastoise's design introduces a technological element to the evolutionary line. Its water cannons resemble the weapons of tanks and armored warships, transforming the turtle's natural defenses into a mechanical and heavily armored form.
- Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: The Squirtle Squad, featured in the Pokémon anime, evokes the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles, a popular franchise during Pokémon's early years. Its name also plays with the word "squad," combining it with "Squirtle" to create the idea of a turtle group.
